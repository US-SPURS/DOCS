# Organizational Structure

The US-SPURS organizational model consists of **Headquarters as Division 0 / 0000** and **13 operating divisions**. Headquarters provides agency-wide direction, governance, enterprise services, and cross-division coordination. The operating divisions execute specialized missions while remaining capable of forming joint task forces and unified-response structures.

## Headquarters

### Division 0 / 0000 — Headquarters, US-SPURS

Headquarters is the central command, governance, policy, and enterprise-coordination element.

**Principal headquarters:**

1000 Patriot Way  
Joint Force Base Freedom Island  
Alameda, California 94500-4501

Joint Force Base Freedom Island is identified in US-SPURS organizational records as the principal headquarters site and as the successor designation for the former Alameda Naval Air Station.

Headquarters responsibilities include:

- strategic direction and agency-wide priorities
- mission authorization and executive decision-making
- policy, doctrine, legal review, and accountability
- enterprise finance, workforce, procurement, and administration
- national, regional, state, territorial, and international coordination
- technology, security, data, infrastructure, and operational standards
- oversight of joint missions involving multiple divisions
- continuity of operations and institutional resilience

## Executive Leadership

### Director / Head of Agency

The Director is the principal executive officer of US-SPURS and holds agency-wide responsibility for strategic direction, mission authorization, executive oversight, and institutional accountability.

**Current Director:** Philip A. Cotton Jr.

### Office of the Director

The Office of the Director supports executive command, policy coordination, intergovernmental affairs, strategic planning, special assignments, and oversight of matters that cross division boundaries.

Titles such as Deputy Director, Chief Operations Officer, Chief Technology Officer, or Chief Infrastructure Officer should not be treated as permanently established executive offices unless separately created, appointed, and documented through controlling policy.

Headquarters may establish executive offices, chiefs, directors, boards, councils, task forces, and program offices as needed without changing the 13-division structure.

## Operating Divisions

### Division 1 / 1000 — Emergency Management

Leads all-hazards preparedness, emergency response, incident coordination, stabilization, recovery support, and unified-response operations.

Primary responsibilities include:

- emergency operations and incident management
- disaster relief and recovery coordination
- mobile command and emergency-support deployments
- continuity and community stabilization
- support to federal, state, tribal, territorial, and local responders

### Division 2 / 2000 — Infrastructure & Public Works

Plans, designs, constructs, restores, and modernizes critical infrastructure and public works systems.

Primary responsibilities include:

- roads, bridges, transit, ports, airfields, and public facilities
- water, wastewater, drainage, and utility systems
- construction, demolition, remediation, and capital projects
- resilient urban and rural infrastructure
- Joint Force Base Freedom Island reconstruction and development
- engineering standards, inspections, and technical field support

### Division 3 / 3000 — Military Support

Coordinates military-support functions, readiness, defense-related logistics, and integration of assigned military personnel who retain their Department of Defense status.

Primary responsibilities include:

- ground, air, sea, and space support capabilities
- operational readiness and mobilization
- defense support to civil authorities
- military engineering and logistics support
- exercises, training, and mission certification

### Division 4 / 4000 — Law Enforcement & Investigations

Provides law-enforcement, investigative, protective, inspection, and public-safety capabilities within authorized jurisdiction.

Primary responsibilities include:

- criminal, administrative, and special investigations
- protective services and facility security
- fraud, waste, abuse, and misconduct investigations
- evidence, intelligence, and case-management support
- assistance to police, fire, EMS, and public-safety partners

### Division 5 / 5000 — Health & Human Services

Coordinates medical, dental, vision, behavioral-health, public-health, and direct human-services programs.

Primary responsibilities include:

- mobile medical, dental, and vision services
- emergency and field healthcare support
- healthcare access and voucher programs
- public-health readiness and response
- veteran, family, disability, and community-support services

### Division 6 / 6000 — Education & Workforce Development

Operates and supports education, vocational training, professional development, youth programs, and workforce pipelines.

Primary responsibilities include:

- United States Academy programs
- K–12, adult diploma, vocational, college, and continuing education
- leadership and technical training
- at-risk and adverse-youth programs
- Youth Unleashed Program administration
- tuition support and employee development

### Division 7 / 7000 — Housing & Homelessness

Coordinates housing, shelter, homelessness response, relocation assistance, community recovery, and long-term stabilization programs.

Primary responsibilities include:

- emergency and transitional housing
- homelessness outreach and direct assistance
- shelter, sanitation, and community-support systems
- residential and business relocation programs
- local community-improvement initiatives
- post-disaster housing and neighborhood recovery

### Division 8 / 8000 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI

Leads research, development, advanced computing, artificial intelligence, digital systems, cybersecurity, and technology transition.

Primary responsibilities include:

- ATLANTIS AI Multi-AI System
- SWARM as an integrated ATLANTIS module
- applied research and engineering prototypes
- software platforms, automation, robotics, and autonomous systems
- cybersecurity, data platforms, and advanced analytics
- laboratory, university, and technology partnerships
- transition of experimental capabilities into operational service

### Division 9 / 9000 — Space, Aviation, Maritime & Mobility

Develops and coordinates space, aviation, maritime, unmanned-system, and advanced-mobility missions and infrastructure.

Primary responsibilities include:

- space partnerships and launch-support programs
- airfield, runway, vertical-pad, and aviation operations
- shipyard and maritime-support development
- unmanned aerial, surface, and subsurface systems
- advanced transportation and mobility research
- space-center and launch-site development

### Division 10 / 10000 — Energy & Environmental Resilience

Develops resilient energy systems, environmental programs, remediation projects, and climate-adaptation capabilities.

Primary responsibilities include:

- microgrids, backup power, and distributed energy
- renewable and advanced-energy systems
- environmental cleanup and hazardous-site remediation
- endangered-species protection and relocation coordination
- climate resilience and sustainability projects

### Division 11 / 11000 — Logistics & Procurement

Provides agency-wide logistics, purchasing, warehousing, transportation, fleet, equipment, and supply-chain management.

Primary responsibilities include:

- procurement and contracting support
- inventory, warehousing, and distribution
- fleet, fuel, aviation, maritime, and equipment logistics
- emergency supply-chain operations
- field sustainment and deployment support
- accountable-property and asset management

### Division 12 / 12000 — Finance & Enterprise Services

Provides shared administrative, financial, and business services across Headquarters and all operating divisions.

Primary responsibilities include:

- budgeting, accounting, financial controls, and reimbursements
- human resources, payroll, benefits, and workforce administration
- records, correspondence, policy administration, and internal services
- procurement governance and contract administration
- enterprise facilities and administrative support
- coordination with US-SPURS financial-service entities where applicable

### Division 13 / 13000 — Strategic Affairs & Global Operations

Coordinates strategic projects, intergovernmental relations, external partnerships, regional alignment, and international operations.

Primary responsibilities include:

- federal, state, tribal, territorial, and local coordination
- international and allied-partner engagement
- strategic special projects that cross division boundaries
- regional and state-office oversight
- external affairs and institutional partnerships
- global humanitarian and infrastructure-support missions

## Geographic Command Structure

US-SPURS uses a layered geographic structure subordinate to Headquarters.

### Priority Order

Unless superseded by a controlling emergency or mission directive, organizational priorities are ranked:

1. Alameda County
2. California
3. United States
4. U.S. Territories and Allies
5. Earth
6. The Great Beyond

### Regional Offices

Regional offices coordinate multi-state operations, readiness, resource allocation, and support between Headquarters and state or territorial offices.

### State and Territorial Offices

The organizational model provides for one satellite office per U.S. state and territory, with those offices coordinating local programs, field activity, partner relationships, and regional reporting.

### International Offices and Missions

International offices, liaison elements, project teams, and temporary mission units may be established for humanitarian, infrastructure, research, allied-support, and strategic operations.

## Command and Coordination Model

Each operating division is led by an appointed division head or commanding official who reports through the command structure established by the Director.

US-SPURS missions generally operate through one of three models.

### Single-Division Mission

One division holds primary responsibility and receives support from Headquarters or other divisions.

### Joint Mission

Two or more divisions operate under a designated lead division, mission commander, or joint task-force structure.

### Unified Response Mission

US-SPURS integrates its divisions with federal, state, tribal, territorial, local, military, nonprofit, private-sector, or international partners through an Incident Command System or another approved unified-command framework.

## Example: Major Disaster Response

A major disaster response may involve:

- Division 1 — Emergency Management
- Division 2 — Infrastructure & Public Works
- Division 3 — Military Support
- Division 4 — Law Enforcement & Investigations
- Division 5 — Health & Human Services
- Division 7 — Housing & Homelessness
- Division 8 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI
- Division 10 — Energy & Environmental Resilience
- Division 11 — Logistics & Procurement
- Division 13 — Strategic Affairs & Global Operations

Headquarters retains strategic oversight, resolves cross-division conflicts, authorizes exceptional actions, and maintains agency-wide accountability.

## Operating Principles

US-SPURS is structured around the following principles:

- centralized strategic direction with distributed execution
- unity of command and clarity of responsibility
- cross-division integration instead of institutional silos
- mission-oriented action supported by documented governance
- technical depth combined with operational speed
- scalable support from local missions through global operations
- continuity, resilience, and long-term capability development
- increased agency and autonomy for any natural person or sentient being

## Organizational Control

This page describes the consolidated organizational model. Formal establishment, renaming, consolidation, or dissolution of a division, executive office, regional command, state office, or subordinate program must be documented through an approved directive, charter, reorganization order, or controlling policy.
