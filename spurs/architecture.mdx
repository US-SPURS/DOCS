# Systems Architecture

US-SPURS systems are designed as a modular, resilient, interoperable capability ecosystem rather than a single monolithic platform.

**Division 8 / 8000 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI** is the primary technology architecture owner. Headquarters establishes enterprise policy and governance, while other divisions own mission-specific applications, data, equipment, and operational requirements within the shared architecture.

## Architecture Objectives

The reference architecture should support:

- rapid deployment
- secure interoperability
- degraded-mode and offline operation where required
- modular upgrades and provider substitution
- auditability and traceability
- multi-region resilience
- field-to-command integration
- human oversight and override for high-impact automation

## Architecture Layers

### 1. Experience Layer

Interfaces used by operators, planners, administrators, partners, and the public where applicable.

Examples include dashboards, mobile applications, mission portals, case-management systems, reporting tools, and public-service interfaces.

### 2. Universal Request Normalization / Protocol Translation Layer

Normalizes user, system, API, agent, and machine requests into consistent internal representations for routing and execution.

### 3. Semantic Capability Intelligence Layer

Discovers and evaluates available models, agents, tools, APIs, data sources, workflows, infrastructure, and other capabilities relevant to a request or mission.

### 4. Capability Provisioning / Composition / Synthesis Layer

Selects, assembles, provisions, or synthesizes the capabilities required to complete an authorized task.

### 5. Strategic Multi-AI Arbitration / Routing Layer

Routes work among AI providers, models, tools, services, and specialist agents based on policy, capability, quality, cost, latency, trust, and mission requirements.

### 6. Autonomous Task Graph / Multi-Agent Execution Layer

Coordinates durable task graphs, SWARM multi-agent execution, dependencies, retries, checkpoints, approvals, and result synthesis.

### 7. Service & Integration Layer

Provides reusable capabilities including identity, messaging, logistics, mapping, documents, asset tracking, notifications, APIs, events, MCP servers, and workflow integrations.

### 8. Data & Knowledge Layer

Manages operational data, mission records, telemetry, geospatial information, infrastructure data, knowledge stores, retrieval systems, analytics pipelines, and authorized historical context.

### 9. Infrastructure & Edge Layer

Includes cloud environments, regional and edge compute, mobile systems, local fallback capability, networking, storage, observability, and deployment infrastructure.

### 10. Policy / Identity / Trust Boundary Layer

Applies identity, authorization, encryption, segmentation, policy enforcement, data controls, human approval, monitoring, and incident-response requirements across every layer.

## ATLANTIS AI

ATLANTIS is the enterprise multi-AI orchestration framework spanning capability discovery, routing, execution, policy, observability, and decision support.

ATLANTIS incorporates systems and modules that were previously treated separately, including SWARM and related multi-agent execution capabilities.

## Cross-Division Ownership

Technology architecture is shared through explicit ownership boundaries.

- **Division 0 / Headquarters** — enterprise governance, policy, authorization, standards, and strategic oversight
- **Division 1** — emergency-management requirements, incident systems, operational continuity
- **Division 2** — infrastructure engineering, public works systems, facility technology
- **Division 3** — military-support requirements and operational integration
- **Division 4** — law-enforcement, investigative, evidence, and case-system requirements
- **Division 5** — health and human-services systems
- **Division 6** — education and workforce systems
- **Division 7** — housing, homelessness, and community-service systems
- **Division 8** — enterprise technology architecture, ATLANTIS AI, cybersecurity, data, software, research, and automation
- **Division 9** — space, aviation, maritime, unmanned, and mobility systems
- **Division 10** — energy, environmental, sensing, and resilience systems
- **Division 11** — logistics, procurement, fleet, inventory, and supply-chain systems
- **Division 12** — finance, HR, payroll, records, and enterprise-service systems
- **Division 13** — strategic, regional, intergovernmental, partnership, and global-operation systems

## System Boundary Requirements

Every major production system should define:

- mission purpose
- accountable owner
- authorized users and workloads
- data inputs and outputs
- trust boundaries
- dependencies
- availability and performance requirements
- recovery objectives
- audit and retention requirements
- support and lifecycle model

## Integration Standards

Preferred patterns include:

- documented and versioned APIs
- event-driven interfaces for durable asynchronous coordination
- Model Context Protocol for approved AI-facing tools and resources
- typed schemas and explicit contracts
- workload identity and least-privilege authorization
- idempotent operations where practical
- structured logging, correlation IDs, and distributed tracing
- bounded retry and reconciliation behavior

## Deployment Environments

The deployment model should distinguish:

- development
- integration and automated test
- staging
- production
- restricted or sensitive environments
- field and edge deployments
- isolated recovery or continuity environments where required

## Resilience

Mission-critical systems should be designed for partial failure rather than assuming perfect connectivity or provider availability.

Resilience patterns may include:

- provider failover
- local or edge processing
- durable queues and workflow history
- replicated state where appropriate
- offline operation and delayed synchronization
- tested backup and restoration
- manual fallback procedures

## Security Architecture

Security is an enterprise responsibility led technically by Division 8 and governed by Headquarters.

Architecture should implement:

- identity-first access
- explicit trust boundaries
- least privilege
- encryption in transit and at rest
- secret isolation and rotation
- network and workload segmentation
- audit logging
- dependency and supply-chain controls
- vulnerability management
- incident detection and response

## Architecture Review

Major systems should receive documented review before production deployment for:

- mission suitability
- security and privacy
- reliability and recovery
- scalability
- interoperability
- accessibility
- supportability
- lifecycle cost
- operational usefulness

## Architecture Decision Records

Significant technical decisions must be captured as Architecture Decision Records documenting context, decision, alternatives, consequences, security impact, operational impact, migration, and rollback.
