# Systems Integration Framework

US-SPURS systems should integrate through explicit, versioned contracts rather than hidden dependencies. **Division 8 / 8000 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI** owns enterprise integration architecture, while each mission division owns the business rules, data responsibilities, and operational requirements of its systems.

## Integration Objectives

- provider-neutral interoperability
- secure exchange of data and commands
- durable workflows with audit history
- clear ownership of every interface
- graceful operation during partial outages
- versioned contracts with controlled deprecation
- cross-division traceability

## Ownership Model

Every production integration must identify:

- the lead owning division
- the producing system owner
- the consuming system owner
- Division 8 architecture ownership where shared technology is involved
- Headquarters policy ownership where enterprise rules apply

Cross-division integrations must not rely on implied ownership.

## Integration Patterns

### Synchronous APIs

Use synchronous APIs when a caller requires an immediate response and the operation can complete within a predictable time window.

### Asynchronous Events

Use events for state changes, notifications, long-running operations, and cross-domain coordination. Events should be immutable, timestamped, traceable, and safe to replay.

### Durable Workflows

Long-running missions should use checkpointed workflows with bounded retries, explicit exit conditions, budget controls, and human escalation for genuine exceptions.

### Model Context Protocol

MCP may expose approved tools, data sources, and operational capabilities to ATLANTIS and authorized AI agents. MCP servers must enforce least privilege, publish clear schemas, identify ownership, and define side-effect behavior.

## ATLANTIS Integration Model

ATLANTIS consumes capabilities through typed adapters and policy-controlled tools rather than embedding provider logic throughout the system.

Provider-specific SDKs should remain behind adapters so routing, auditing, budgets, failover, and policy remain consistent.

ATLANTIS-facing capabilities should declare:

- capability name and purpose
- owning division or service
- authorization requirements
- input and output schema
- side effects
- cost and rate constraints
- timeout behavior
- retry safety
- audit requirements
- human-approval requirements

## Contract Requirements

Every production integration should define:

- owning division and team
- purpose and authorized consumers
- request and response schema
- authentication and authorization method
- timeout and retry behavior
- idempotency strategy
- error taxonomy
- versioning and deprecation policy
- observability requirements
- data-classification requirements
- recovery and reconciliation behavior

## Identity and Trust

Integrations must not assume that network location equals trust. Each request should carry verifiable workload or user identity, scoped authorization, and enough context for policy enforcement.

## Data Handling

- Minimize data shared across boundaries.
- Identify the authoritative system of record.
- Classify data before integration design.
- Encrypt protected data in transit and at rest.
- Record access to protected datasets.
- Define retention and deletion behavior.
- Avoid uncontrolled copies of authoritative data.
- Document synchronization and conflict-resolution rules.

## Event Envelope

A standard event should include:

- event identifier
- event type and schema version
- source system and owning division
- correlation and causation identifiers
- creation timestamp
- actor or workload identity
- classification and handling metadata
- payload

## Division Examples

### Division 1 — Emergency Management

Incident systems may publish alerts, resource requests, operational status, and incident-state events.

### Division 2 — Infrastructure & Public Works

Infrastructure systems may publish asset status, project milestones, inspection findings, and utility telemetry.

### Division 4 — Law Enforcement & Investigations

Investigative integrations require stronger evidence-handling, access-control, audit, and chain-of-custody requirements.

### Division 5 — Health & Human Services

Health integrations require appropriate privacy, consent, and protected-data controls.

### Division 8 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI

Division 8 owns shared adapters, AI orchestration interfaces, cybersecurity integrations, data platforms, and common technical services.

### Division 11 — Logistics & Procurement

Logistics integrations may exchange inventory, fleet, shipment, procurement, and asset-state information.

### Division 12 — Finance & Enterprise Services

Finance and workforce integrations require strong transaction integrity, authorization, audit, and records controls.

## Failure Handling

Critical integrations must include:

- timeouts
- bounded retries with backoff
- dead-letter or exception handling
- duplicate protection
- reconciliation procedures
- operator-visible status
- manual recovery paths where mission impact warrants them

## Integration Review Gate

Before production use, an integration should pass:

1. ownership review
2. architecture review
3. cybersecurity review
4. data-governance and privacy review
5. operational-readiness review
6. failure and recovery validation
7. rollback or disablement review

High-impact integrations should also document controlling ADRs and approval authority.
