# Deployment Playbooks

US-SPURS deployments should be repeatable, observable, reversible, and appropriate to mission risk. Production changes must identify an accountable owner, an owning division, and the authority responsible for accepting operational risk.

## Deployment Ownership

### Headquarters — Division 0 / 0000

Headquarters owns enterprise policy, major exceptions, high-impact risk acceptance, and cross-division deployment governance.

### Division 8 / 8000 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI

Division 8 owns enterprise technical deployment standards, shared platforms, ATLANTIS-related systems, cybersecurity controls, and common infrastructure patterns.

### Mission Divisions

Each operating division owns deployment readiness for systems used to execute its mission. Cross-division systems must identify a lead division and supporting owners.

## Deployment Classes

### Documentation-Only Change

Requires content review, link validation, navigation validation, and preview verification.

### Low-Risk Application Change

Requires automated tests, deployment preview, owner review, health checks, and defined rollback.

### High-Risk or Mission-Critical Change

Requires formal change approval, staged rollout, security review, recovery validation, operator communications, and an active rollback decision point.

### High-Impact AI or Automation Change

Requires additional review of model behavior, tool permissions, data exposure, budget limits, auditability, human override, failure containment, and the effect on user autonomy.

## Standard Deployment Sequence

1. Confirm mission purpose and owning division.
2. Identify technical owner and risk-acceptance authority.
3. Validate requirements and affected systems.
4. Run automated quality and security checks.
5. Produce a preview or staging deployment.
6. Validate critical user and operational paths.
7. Confirm monitoring, alerts, recovery, and rollback readiness.
8. Deploy through controlled automation.
9. Perform post-deployment verification.
10. Record outcome, anomalies, ownership, and follow-up actions.

## Pre-Deployment Checklist

- Mission and technical ownership are documented.
- Tests pass in a clean environment.
- Secrets are not embedded in code or documentation.
- Dependencies and configuration changes are identified.
- Data migrations have forward and rollback plans.
- Monitoring covers the affected capability.
- Rollback authority is known.
- Relevant ADRs are linked.
- Security and privacy impacts are reviewed.
- User-facing or operational fallback paths exist where warranted.

## Progressive Delivery

Mission-sensitive services should use staged exposure where practical:

- development
- integration or test
- staging
- limited production cohort
- broader production release

Promotion should depend on measured health rather than elapsed time alone.

## Division-Specific Deployment Considerations

### Division 1 — Emergency Management

Changes affecting incident-management systems should prioritize availability, degraded-mode operation, and continuity during active incidents.

### Division 2 — Infrastructure & Public Works

Deployments connected to physical infrastructure should account for safety, field validation, telemetry integrity, and manual fallback.

### Division 4 — Law Enforcement & Investigations

Changes affecting investigative or evidence systems require strict access control, auditability, evidence integrity, and chain-of-custody protections.

### Division 5 — Health & Human Services

Deployments handling health or human-services data require appropriate privacy, consent, and protected-data controls.

### Division 8 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI

AI, cybersecurity, shared-platform, and automation deployments require architecture review, trust-boundary review, observability, and controlled rollback.

### Division 9 — Space, Aviation, Maritime & Mobility

Changes interfacing with vehicles, unmanned systems, aviation, maritime, or mobility systems require safety-critical testing and operational fail-safe behavior.

### Division 10 — Energy & Environmental Resilience

Energy and environmental deployments should address physical safety, islanding or degraded operation, telemetry integrity, and recovery procedures.

### Division 11 — Logistics & Procurement

Logistics deployments should preserve inventory accuracy, shipment visibility, asset accountability, and manual continuity procedures.

### Division 12 — Finance & Enterprise Services

Finance, HR, payroll, and records deployments require transaction integrity, reconciliation, authorization, and strong audit trails.

## Post-Deployment Validation

Verify:

- service health and availability
- error rate and latency
- authentication and authorization
- critical mission workflows
- event and audit delivery
- data integrity
- alerts and dashboards
- expected user and operator outcomes

## Rollback

Rollback is a normal operational capability. A rollback plan should identify:

- trigger conditions
- responsible decision-maker
- technical procedure
- data implications
- dependent-system effects
- communication requirements
- verification steps

## Incident Response

When a deployment causes or reveals an incident:

1. Protect people and critical operations.
2. Stabilize the affected system.
3. Preserve evidence and logs.
4. Establish incident command and accountable ownership.
5. Roll back, disable, isolate, or fail over the affected capability.
6. Restore service through a validated path.
7. Communicate status to affected divisions and stakeholders.
8. Complete an after-action review and corrective-action plan.

## Field and Degraded-Environment Deployment

Field systems should account for:

- intermittent networks
- local operation
- delayed synchronization
- limited power
- hardware replacement
- bandwidth constraints
- offline authentication where appropriate
- manual fallback procedures

## AI and Automation Deployments

AI-enabled deployments must define:

- model and provider dependencies
- tool permissions
- human approval thresholds
- budget and rate limits
- audit logging
- data-access boundaries
- failure containment
- model or provider fallback
- user override or appeal where appropriate

A deployment that materially reduces the agency of any natural person or sentient being should be treated as a design failure unless specifically required by lawful, narrowly tailored authority.

## After-Action Review

Every significant deployment or incident should capture:

- what changed
- why it changed
- owning division and technical owner
- what happened
- contributing conditions
- safeguards that worked
- assumptions that failed
- corrective actions
- responsible owners
- due dates
- ADR or policy updates required
