# Infrastructure & Presence

US-SPURS uses a hybrid infrastructure model that combines Headquarters, regional and state-level coordination, fixed facilities, logistics nodes, engineering sites, and mobile deployment systems.

## Headquarters — Joint Force Base Freedom Island

US-SPURS organizational records identify **Joint Force Base Freedom Island**, the successor designation for the former Alameda Naval Air Station, as the principal headquarters site.

**Address:**

1000 Patriot Way  
Joint Force Base Freedom Island  
Alameda, California 94500-4501

Headquarters is organized as **Division 0 / 0000** and provides strategic command, policy, governance, enterprise services, and cross-division coordination.

### Headquarters Functions

- strategic command and mission planning
- executive and administrative operations
- enterprise policy and governance
- technology and data operations support
- intergovernmental and partner coordination
- continuity of operations
- cross-division mission oversight

## Joint Force Base Freedom Island Development

Infrastructure development associated with Joint Force Base Freedom Island is primarily supported by **Division 2 / 2000 — Infrastructure & Public Works**, with major supporting roles for:

- Division 3 — Military Support
- Division 8 — Science, Technology & ATLANTIS AI
- Division 9 — Space, Aviation, Maritime & Mobility
- Division 10 — Energy & Environmental Resilience
- Division 11 — Logistics & Procurement

Program concepts and projects may include:

- runway and airfield improvements
- vertical pads and aviation support areas
- subsurface or hardened facilities
- emergency-launch and fire-suppression systems
- shipyard reconstruction
- space-center and launch-site development
- tramway and internal transportation systems
- medical and veteran-support facilities
- utility modernization
- environmental cleanup and remediation

## Regional Operations Structure

Regional offices coordinate multi-state operations, readiness, resources, and support between Headquarters and state or territorial offices.

Regional coordination is principally aligned through **Division 13 / 13000 — Strategic Affairs & Global Operations**, while mission-specific operational control remains with the responsible lead division.

### Regional Functions

- multi-state mission coordination
- readiness and resource planning
- deployment support
- regional partner coordination
- state and territorial office support
- escalation to Headquarters

## State and Territorial Offices

The organizational model provides for one satellite office in each U.S. state and territory.

These offices may support:

- local mission coordination
- community and partner engagement
- program delivery
- logistics and field support
- regional reporting
- coordination with state, tribal, territorial, and local authorities

## International Offices and Mission Elements

International offices, liaison teams, project groups, and temporary mission elements may be established for humanitarian, infrastructure, research, allied-support, and strategic operations.

Division 13 provides strategic and intergovernmental coordination while the appropriate operating division retains technical or operational ownership.

## Mobile Deployment Systems

US-SPURS maintains or develops deployable systems that can support field operations outside fixed facilities.

Examples include:

- mobile command units
- mobile health, dental, and vision units
- communications vehicles and field networking systems
- temporary power and microgrid systems
- water purification and field utility systems
- temporary housing and stabilization kits
- mobile logistics and supply-distribution capability
- field compute and ATLANTIS-enabled coordination systems

## Engineering & Innovation Facilities

Dedicated engineering, research, testing, and training environments may be used to build and validate capabilities before operational deployment.

Primary division ownership normally includes:

- Division 2 for infrastructure engineering and public works
- Division 8 for research, software, AI, cybersecurity, and advanced technology
- Division 9 for aviation, maritime, space, unmanned systems, and mobility
- Division 10 for energy, environmental, and resilience systems

## Logistics Infrastructure

**Division 11 / 11000 — Logistics & Procurement** leads agency-wide logistics infrastructure, including:

- warehouses
- inventory and distribution systems
- fleet and equipment staging
- fuel and supply operations
- emergency supply-chain support
- accountable-property systems

## Partnerships

US-SPURS infrastructure work may involve public, private, academic, nonprofit, military, and international partners.

### Government and Public-Sector Coordination

May include federal, state, tribal, territorial, and local entities; emergency-management organizations; public infrastructure authorities; and military-support partners.

### Private-Sector Collaboration

May include engineering, construction, technology, manufacturing, utility, logistics, transportation, and infrastructure organizations.

### Academic and Research Collaboration

May include universities, applied-research centers, laboratories, training institutions, and technical workforce partners.

Partnership references in this documentation describe coordination categories and do not by themselves imply endorsement, contractual status, or formal affiliation with any specific external entity.

## Infrastructure Priority Framework

Unless superseded by a controlling mission or emergency directive, organizational geographic priorities are:

1. Alameda County
2. California
3. United States
4. U.S. Territories and Allies
5. Earth
6. The Great Beyond

## Physical Deployment Philosophy

US-SPURS treats physical infrastructure as an integrated capability system rather than simply a collection of buildings.

The infrastructure footprint should enable:

- command and coordination
- engineering and public works
- logistics and mobility
- healthcare and human services
- research and technology development
- emergency response
- resilient energy and utilities
- long-term community stabilization

## Infrastructure Governance

Facilities, bases, regional offices, capital projects, and major infrastructure programs should have documented ownership, mission purpose, funding authority, lifecycle responsibility, security requirements, and operational-support plans.
